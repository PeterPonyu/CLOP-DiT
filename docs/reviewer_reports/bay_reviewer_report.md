# Reviewer Report

## Title: CLOP-DiT: Text-Guided Single-Cell Gene Expression Generation via Contrastive Language-Omics Pretraining and Diffusion Transformers

### 1. General Comments
The manuscript presents a novel and interesting approach to generating single-cell gene expression profiles conditioned on natural-language descriptions using a two-stage pipeline (CLOP and DiT). The methodology is sound, and the combination of Contrastive Language-Omics Pretraining with Diffusion Transformers is an innovative step for the field of computational biology. However, there are several areas regarding visualization policies, figure presentations, and writing style that need to be addressed to improve the clarity, readability, and overall impact of the paper.

### 2. Visualization Policies and Figure Presentations
*   **Missing In-Panel Legends:** In Figure 6 (Marker gene comparison), the caption states, "the lineage colour mapping is described in the caption rather than repeated as an in-panel legend." This is highly discouraged. Forcing readers to continuously switch between the figure and the caption to understand color encodings disrupts the reading flow and reduces the figure's standalone comprehensibility. Please include a clear, in-panel legend for lineage colors.
*   **Redundancy in Data Visualization:** In Figure 15 (DE concordance), the caption mentions that a "grouped-bar panel visualises the same per-contrast scores to aid comparison" alongside a summary heatmap. Presenting the exact same data in two different visual formats within the same figure is redundant and wastes valuable space. The authors should choose the most effective visualization (likely the heatmap or the grouped bars, but not both) and remove the other.
*   **Clarification of Baselines in Figures vs. Text:** In Section 3.8 (Baseline Comparison), the text mentions "shuffled-label and mean-collapse controls" as part of the benchmarking pipeline. However, the caption for Figure 12 only lists "unconditional CFG=0, Gaussian per-type sampling, decoder-only". The text and the figure must be perfectly aligned. If those controls are in the benchmark, they should be clearly visible and labeled in the figure, or the text should be adjusted to reflect exactly what is plotted.
*   **Figure Complexity and Legibility:** Several figures (e.g., Figure 5, Figure 14) contain multiple complex sub-panels (scatter plots, outlier profiles, heatmaps, ROC curves). The authors must ensure that all axis labels, tick marks, and annotations remain strictly legible when printed at the journal's standard column or page width. 
*   **Treatment of Fréchet Distance (FD):** The authors rightly note in the Discussion that Fréchet Distance can be misleading for conditional generation tasks. However, FD is introduced much earlier in Table 1 and Figure 4. It would be highly beneficial to add a brief caveat in the Results section when FD is first presented, rather than waiting until the Discussion, so readers do not misinterpret the metric during their initial read.

### 3. Results Presentation
*   **Validation Split Representativeness:** The methodology mentions an 80/20 study-level split (72 training datasets, 8 held-out validation datasets). It would strengthen the manuscript to briefly verify or state in the text whether these 8 held-out datasets encompass a representative distribution of the cell types, tissues, and biological conditions present in the training set, to fully support the generalizability claims.
*   **Contextualizing CLOP Validation Accuracy:** The training dynamics section mentions a CLOP validation accuracy of "~2.5%" and contextualizes it as "6.4× random chance". While factually correct, 2.5% absolute accuracy sounds very low to a general reader. The authors should briefly expand on *why* this absolute value is sufficient to produce the well-separated condition space necessary for the DiT, perhaps by contrasting the difficulty of the 256-way (or batch-size-way) contrastive task.

### 4. Article Writings
*   **Repetitive and Formulaic Transitions:** The opening sentences of almost every subsection in the Results are highly formulaic (e.g., "This subsection summarizes...", "This subsection visualizes...", "This subsection reports..."). This reads more like an outline than a cohesive scientific narrative. The authors should revise these transitions to flow more naturally, focusing on the scientific motivation for the next set of experiments rather than explicitly stating the structural purpose of the paragraph.
*   **Dense Phrasing:** Some sentences are overly dense and could be broken up for better readability. For instance, in the Abstract: *"Across 100 cell types from 80 datasets (220,304 cells, 1,088 text groups), CLOP-DiT achieves k-nearest-neighbor accuracy of 36.9% (37× random chance with 100 classes), steering accuracy of 81.0% (random = 50%), and a diversity ratio of 0.93 under the recommended Midpoint solver at CFG 1.0."* Consider streamlining this to ensure the core achievements are easily digestible.
*   **Informal Phrasing:** In Section 3.9, the phrase *"making clear that downstream performance is strongly heterogeneous by cell type"* is slightly informal. Consider more academic phrasing such as *"demonstrating that downstream performance exhibits strong heterogeneity across cell types."*

### 5. Conclusion
The manuscript is a strong contribution to single-cell generative modeling. Addressing the concerns regarding figure legends, visualization redundancy, and the narrative flow in the Results section will significantly improve the manuscript's clarity and make it suitable for publication.
