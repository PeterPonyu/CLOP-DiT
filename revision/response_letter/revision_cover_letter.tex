\section*{Summary of Revision Work}

In response to the 15 reviewer comments (11 from Reviewer~\#2, 4 from Reviewer~\#3), we completed
\textbf{eleven new analyses and experiments} organised into four revision lanes:

\subsection*{Lane A\,---\,Post-hoc analyses (5 completed, no retraining required)}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A1} (Comment~2.6): Latent-space KNN error taxonomy over a 10-family biological
    classification. Key finding: 49.2\% of mis-typings stay within the same biological family;
    cross-lineage failures are the minority.
  \item \textbf{A2} (Comment~3.2): Organism-stratified evaluation across 18 human-only and
    8 mouse-only cell types. Key finding: no statistically significant species gap ($p > 0.14$
    on all headline metrics).
  \item \textbf{A3} (Comment~2.7): Abundance vs.\ fidelity analysis replacing the vague phrase
    ``biologically difficult.'' Key finding: within-type heterogeneity ($\rho = -0.77$), not
    training-set abundance ($\rho = +0.57$), is the dominant predictor of per-type fidelity.
  \item \textbf{A4} (Comment~2.8): Gene-level variance quantification with explicit tolerance-band
    analysis. Key finding: 85\% of genes within the $[0.5, 2.0]$ tolerance band;
    within-type median $r_\text{var} = +0.85$.
  \item \textbf{A5} (Comment~2.11): Rare-cell failure mechanism decomposed at latent and
    expression scales. Key finding: upstream latent under-dispersion (50\% of real), not decoder
    collapse; the scGPT decoder is already expansive.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Lane B\,---\,Retraining and bridge experiments (4 completed)}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{B1} (Comment~2.9): Gaussian variance-recovery baselines at both latent and
    expression scales, bracketing CLOP-DiT between a type-agnostic floor ($r_\text{var} = +0.014$)
    and a type-aware oracle ($+0.813$).
  \item \textbf{B2} (Comment~3.4): Formal ZCA whitening ablation (3 CLOP training runs). Key
    finding: 1.0\% quality drop without ZCA; ZCA is a modest refinement, not a critical component.
  \item \textbf{B3} (Comment~3.3): Augmentation strategy sweep ($2 \times 6 \times 4$ grid) plus
    forced-scarcity positive control. Key finding: augmentation provides genuine value under
    artificial scarcity (F1 improvement $+$0.11--$+$0.36) but not at the natural performance
    ceiling.
  \item \textbf{B4} (Comment~2.10): CLOP$\to$full-pipeline bridge with 3 DiT retrains. Key
    finding: Stage-1 ablation rankings are only a partial-reversal proxy for end-to-end quality;
    the cohesion loss is essential for inter-type geometric structure.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Lane D\,---\,Encoder comparison (completed)}

\begin{itemize}
  \item scGPT vs.\ PCA encoder comparison on 8 representative datasets. Key finding:
    within-type L2 compression is scGPT-architecture-specific (7--15$\times$), not a
    universal property of dimensionality reduction.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Lane C\,---\,Zero-shot strict-OOD evaluation (completed)}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Lane C} (Comment~3.1): Ingested three strict-OOD tissues via the CellxGene
    Census API---kidney (Tabula Sapiens, 3{,}406 post-QC cells), cerebellum (human brain
    atlas, 19{,}981 cells, 7{,}367 Purkinje cells), and fetal gonadal tissue (3{,}342 cells,
    294 Leydig $+$ 3{,}048 adrenal cortex type~I). Verified zero leakage across nine
    training-metadata files, then ran a zero-shot evaluation of the production CLOP-DiT (no
    retraining). Key finding: the model partially generalises with sharp program-dependent
    variance. Nearest-real-centroid accuracy is $3$--$6\times$ random for structurally-familiar
    novel types (adrenal cortex $= 0.875$, kidney epithelial $= 0.460$) but at-or-below random
    for structurally-distinct novel types (Purkinje $= 0.00$, cerebellar interneurons $= 0.00$).
    This is a mechanistic ceiling result identifying training-corpus coverage at the cell-program
    level as the limiting factor, not architecture or decoder capacity. Reported in
    Section~3.13 with per-cell-type figure and summary table (Table~S13).
\end{itemize}
