revision round. After converting the original Lane-C scope into an
explicit go/no-go gate, the full lane was rejected as too expensive for
the remaining reviewer delta, and the narrowed strict-OOD-only fallback
also remained closed because the required staffing/readiness conditions
were not met (no committed `<= 18 engineer-days` staffing confirmation,
only provisional owner slots rather than an approved staffing decision
for the B-2 strict-OOD leakage check and the B-3 label-bridge review,
and no populated / owned revision-side
execution pack, even though the repo now contains technical
scaffolding for the ingest directory, leakage checker, and label-bridge
templates). We therefore treat strict-OOD retraining as an explicit
future-work item rather than an unacknowledged omission. This does not
erase the current evidence base: A2 already shows no type-level species
bias, A3 shows that within-type heterogeneity rather than raw abundance
is the dominant error axis, B3-forced-scarcity shows that naive
augmentation-by-mixing does not recover the missing headroom, and B4
shows that stage-1 CLOP improvements do not automatically transfer to
the full end-to-end pipeline. In other words, the remaining Lane-C gap
is now a clearly scoped strict-OOD generalisation question, not an
ambiguous broad data-scaling deficit.

strongest remaining way to answer Reviewer Comment R3.1. However, after
quantifying the real cost of that lane, we explicitly closed the full
Lane-C plan for this revision round and only kept a narrowed

revision posture of retaining the no-go decision and stating the
limitation explicitly. Importantly, this does not leave the reviewer
question completely unsupported: A2 already addresses the species-bias
part at the type level, A3 shows that heterogeneity rather than
abundance is the dominant failure axis, B3-forced-scarcity shows that
simple augmentation does not solve that axis, and B4 shows that partial
stage gains do not automatically justify a full-pipeline retrain. We
therefore frame strict-OOD retraining as the next targeted release-step,
not as a result we can responsibly claim to have finished here.
